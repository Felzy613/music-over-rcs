# Zemer and YouTube Music

A `search` looks first in **Zemer's catalog**, the service the [Zemer](https://github.com/ZemerTeam/zemer-app) Android app searches with (`search.zemer.io`), and on [music-table.com](music-table.md) (your catalog, then the site) only when Zemer has nothing that fits. Zemer has whole albums as well as singles; music-table.com mostly has new singles, so it is the fallback. The songs themselves are YouTube Music's: a Zemer song's id is its YouTube video id, and its audio comes from YouTube Music's own app API (InnerTube), the way the Zemer app plays it. Everything is held to Zemer's artist list, with female singers excluded. The code is `src/sources/zemer.ts` (the catalog), `src/sources/youtube-music.ts` (the audio, and YouTube Music's own search for when Zemer can't be reached) and `src/sources/zemer-whitelist.ts` (the list).

## Searching Zemer

- **Why Zemer and not YouTube Music's search.** YouTube Music searches all of YouTube Music: "lipa" brings Dua Lipa there, and nothing in its first page passes Zemer's list. Zemer's catalog holds only what the list allows, already sorted into songs, videos, albums, singles and artists, and it reads English spellings of Hebrew names ("elazar lipa'le" finds the album "אלעזר ליפא׳לע"). It answers in about a fifth of a second. Every request says what to leave out, because the server leaves in whatever isn't said: `allowFemale=0`, `blockVideos=0`, `kidZone=0`, as the app sends them (`ZemerSearchClient.kt`).
- **What it asks for.** 50 of each kind (songs, albums, singles, videos; a kind with fewer comes whole): Zemer's order is not by plays, and at 20 "achas" missed its most played song (1.5 million plays). It answered as fast at 50 as at 20 (0.16 to 0.28 s), and an answer is kept 10 minutes, so `songs more` asks nothing new.
- **What counts as an answer.** Zemer answers any words with something (nonsense brings whatever shares a letter or two), so the bot takes only what has **every** word you typed in its title or artist, an apostrophe or a small typo forgiven, and an English spelling read against Hebrew letters (`soundsLike` in `src/sources/relevance.ts`: "vnusni" for "ונתנו", "shabbos" for "שבת"). An artist counts when every word is in their name (either alphabet) and the words cover at least half of it. A near miss ("higale na" is not "Hava Nagila") is a different song, so music-table.com gets to answer.
- **A menu, never a pick** (the owner's design, 2026-10-20; `src/ranking.ts`, `src/menu.ts`, [Using the bot](using-the-bot.md#a-searchs-menu)). What fits is listed, up to five artists, five albums and five songs, and nothing is sent or opened on its own: not the one song the words name, not an artist's whole name. The order: **artists** spelled exactly by the words, then started by them, then a typo away ("achas" offers Nachas, last); **songs** the words name (the title, give or take "(feat. …)", "(Live)" and the alphabet: "Achas אחת", "Forever - פוראבר"), then those with every word in the title (the artist's own name in a title doesn't count), then those that fit only with the artist's name, the most played first within each, a song before a video of it; **albums** the words name, then the newest (year, then release date), then the bigger, an album's editions as one line. Duplicates are folded keeping the most played copy (the first copy of Mordechai Shapiro's "Achas" had no play count, the second 1.5 million). A single is a song here (a "single" of several songs opens like an album); one a song found already has ("Achas (feat. …)" for "Achas") is left out; a video is a song only when no song has it. A 👍 on an artist opens their page (their newest albums, editions folded, and their most played songs, a song listed even when its album is); on an album, its songs.
- **The lines.** An artist is `🎤 Name`, an album `💿 2. Title — Artist · 2008 · 14 songs`, a song `🎵 Title — Artist`; " — Artist" is left off when the menu's only artist is the line's (a long credit is cut to "A & 7 more"). Songs have no numbers (👍 picks); albums do, running on across pages, and texting one opens it: its songs numbered as on the album, then `📦 All 12 songs as one zip · 44 min` (a 👍 there, or `zip`, gets the zip). A menu ends with a line on what `… more` can still show and what an album's number does, which has no mark: it is not a song, and starts with an invisible left-to-right mark the runner knows as the bot's. The 💿, 🎤 and 📦 lines start with their own mark instead of the bot's 🎵, which the runner knows as the bot's too. A line with Hebrew in it gets invisible left-to-right marks (`markBotText` in `src/runner.ts`), because a phone lays out a message whose first letter is Hebrew right to left and pulls a year that follows a Hebrew title into it ("15 · 2025 · אלעזר ליפא׳לע songs").
- **Pictures.** An artist's songs come with their cover. The search sends none with a song, so it takes the cover of the album or single found with it; failing that, when the song is sent, its album's page is asked for its cover (one request, kept 10 minutes, so sending the album next asks nothing). That is asked while the song downloads, not before: the card is drawn once the cover is in, and the file's tags wait for the album's details. Only when that fails too is it YouTube's video picture (the cover with bars around it).
- **When Zemer's search can't be reached** (an error, no answer within 10 seconds, or `ZEMER_SEARCH=off`), YouTube Music's own search answers instead, held to the same list and to every word (its songs, and its albums with Zemer's album filter); with neither, the catalog and music-table.com answer. At start the bot asks Zemer once and logs whether it answers ("Zemer's search answers").
- **`zemer` and a song** (also `youtube`, `yt`) asks Zemer alone, with nothing to fall back on.
- **`album`, `artist` or `song` and a name** is a menu of that kind alone. Opening an album (a 👍 on its 💿 line, or its number) asks Zemer for its page (`/album`): its cover and every song in order. The album must pass the list by its artist's id, and each song on its own: a song credited to the album's artist (or to no one) is theirs, a guest is looked up on the list by name, and a song that doesn't pass (a woman's, say) is left out and counted ("12 songs, coming as a zip (1 left out)"). An album Zemer doesn't have is opened on YouTube Music instead.

## New releases from Zemer

`new`, the daily message and the alerts for artists you follow list **Zemer's own new releases** beside music-table.com's (the owner's request, 2026-10-28). They come from the feed behind the *Latest Releases* shelf of the Zemer app's home tab (`docs/latest_releases` in the [Zemer repository](https://github.com/ZemerTeam/zemer-app); `src/sources/zemer-releases.ts`). YouTube's own "new releases" carry almost no music from the artists on Zemer's list, which is why Zemer builds this feed:

- **Where it is.** `GET https://flipphoneguy.duckdns.org/?page=zemer_releases`: a personal server of a Zemer team member, **not** `search.zemer.io` (the app's `LatestReleasesStore.FEED_URL`). A job there walks every listed artist's discography once an hour and writes one small JSON (about 20 KB, an ETag, `Cache-Control: max-age=900`) of the releases of the last 14 days, newest first: `{ generatedAt, whitelistVersion, windowDays, count, releases }`, each release `{ artistId, artistName, title, browseId, playlistId, thumbnail, year, uploadDate, trackCount, sampleVideoId }`. `browseId` is the album id that `/album` opens, `uploadDate` (ISO, with an offset) is when it went up, `trackCount` 1 marks a single, and `year` is the catalog's year, which differs for a re-upload (an old album put up again is "new" here, as in the app).
- **How the bot reads it.** The host asks with the last answer's ETag (a 304 costs nothing), at most every 30 minutes, and `new` asks again first when the last answer is over 15 minutes old (waiting at most 4 seconds for it, so a slow feed never holds up the list). The releases are kept in the catalog (`zemer_releases`, for 45 days) with when each was first seen, announced and alerted. A row missing an id, title, artist or date is skipped and the rest of the feed counts; a title over 200 characters is dropped; a cover is taken only from Google's picture hosts (`googleusercontent.com`, `ggpht.com`, `ytimg.com`), else the release has no picture.
- **The artist list is applied to it**, as to everything from Zemer: a release is kept and listed only if its artist's channel id is on the list, no one credited is flagged female, and neither the album nor a single's song is blocked; the check is made when it is stored and again whenever it is listed, announced or alerted. Nothing about a release is stored as a song. A single's line is `single:<album id>`, which opens the album's page on Zemer (like a single in a search menu), checks every song again and sends it; an album's is `album:<id>`. So everything after the 👍 is the code a menu already uses, including the check made when a song is sent.
- **Both places, once.** A song or album that both Zemer's feed and music-table.com have (the same title and artist, a single against a single and an album against an album, "(feat. …)" ignored) is listed once, as Zemer's: in `new`, the other is left out; in the daily message, music-table.com's post is left out and counts as announced, so it does not turn up the next day. A song Zemer lists after music-table.com announced it is announced again, as Zemer's.
- **The alerts** match an artist you follow by their name in the feed, in the list's other alphabet, or in the list's name for them, and are for releases first seen since you started following and no older than two days, none of whose songs you've had.
- **It can break, and the bot carries on.** It is an unofficial, hosted-by-a-person feed, so it can move or stop; the app itself would lose its shelf the same way. A failed read is logged once (`could not read Zemer's new releases…`) and tried again after ten minutes; `new` and the daily message are music-table.com's alone meanwhile. After 12 hours of failing, a problem is raised (mailed, on the cloud host) and cleared when the feed is read again. If the app's address changes (`FEED_URL` in `LatestReleasesStore.kt`), set `ZEMER_RELEASES_URL`; `ZEMER_RELEASES=off` stops using it.
- **Checking it by hand.** `npm run library -- sync` reads it now and `npm run library -- status` shows how many are kept and the newest; `npm run library -- digest --days 3` shows the daily message with them in it. (`content.zemer.io` answered the Mac this was built on with Cloudflare's 403 page on 2026-10-29, so there the artist list did not load and nothing from Zemer is listed, since nothing is listed without the list.)

## Getting the songs

- **On a host YouTube turns away.** YouTube's player answers many datacenter addresses with "Sign in to confirm you're not a bot", whatever the client; the cloud host's is one (seen on Render on 2026-10-16, every ask for every song). Searches and album pages still work from there; only the player and its media links are refused. So on the cloud host the songs come through a relay (next item). Without one, when every ask for a song meets the bot check, `YouTubeMusic` marks the host as turned away for 15 minutes and logs it once: meanwhile a `search` goes straight to music-table.com instead of offering songs that can't be sent, and `zemer` and `album` say "YouTube Music is turning this server away for now". At start the bot asks for one short, well-known video (`checkStreams`) and logs whether songs can be had (from the host, or through the relay), and every failed YouTube step (search, album page, the player, a refused link, a download, the relay) is logged with its reason. Zemer's own way around this, its relay `stream.zemer.io`, serves only Zemer's filtered-device users (it answers "not available" to anyone else), so it isn't used.
- **The relay** (`YOUTUBE_RELAY=r2`, which the cloud host sets for itself; see [the cloud host](cloud.md)). The Cloudflare Worker beside the host fetches each song from Cloudflare's addresses, which YouTube does serve (tried 2026-10-16: the player said OK and whole songs downloaded). The host asks by leaving a request in the R2 bucket, the Worker asks the player and downloads the song into the bucket the way `YouTubeMusic` would (the same client versions, a fresh session on a refused link, the same `Range` request), and the host picks it up (`src/cloud/youtube-relay.ts`; the two never call each other, as with the alerts). In `src/sources/youtube-music.ts` it is the `relay` option: every song is fetched through it and held to the same rules as a download from here (audio, not empty, within `MAX_DOWNLOAD_MB`), and the player is never asked from the host. A song is then checked before it is sent without asking anyone (the relay's only answer is the song itself), so its size is known only once it arrives; an album's songs are all on disk before its zip is made, so that doesn't matter. Zemer's list is still applied on the host, at search and again just before sending, exactly as without a relay.
- **When YouTube turns the relay away too.** From Cloudflare's network the bot check comes and goes, a minute or two at a time. On 2026-10-18 it met the start-up check at 02:37, and a second ask made straight after, with a new session, met it too. Of asks made every 15 to 30 seconds from 02:41, the first 14 came through; then for about a minute and a half most were turned away (the bot check, or `HTTP 403` from YouTube Music, which took about 8 seconds where a song takes 1 to 1.5), though one in the middle came through; from 02:48:39 they came through again. At one ask a minute from 02:51, 7 of the first 8 came through (the one turned away, at 02:54, was followed 25 seconds later by one that came through), then from 03:00 a spell of `HTTP 403` and bot checks lasted over two minutes. In all, about one ask in four between 02:41 and 03:02 was turned away, always in spells. An ask made straight after a turned-away one was turned away too; one made 15 seconds or more later often wasn't. So:
  - The Worker counts the bot check, and `HTTP 403` or `429` from YouTube Music, as being turned away (`turnedAway` in its answer). A 403 or 429 ends that ask at once: it is the address YouTube refuses, not the client version. A retired version (`HTTP 400`) is passed over for the next, as on the host. It doesn't ask again itself, since asking at once doesn't help.
  - The host asks again 5 seconds later, and if that is turned away too, 15 seconds after that (`RETRY_WAITS_MS` in `src/cloud/youtube-relay.ts`). Each ask is a new request to the Worker, with a new session.
  - Only when all three are turned away are YouTube songs left aside, and through a relay only for **1 minute**: at the end of it the relay is asked for the start-up check's short video, and the moment that comes through, YouTube songs are back ("YouTube Music lets the relay through again" in the log). If it is turned away again, they are left aside twice as long, 2, 4 and 8 minutes, then 15 at a time, each ending with another check (`RELAY_TURNED_AWAY_MS` in `src/sources/youtube-music.ts`). Songs asked for together (an album's) count as one time. A start-up check that is turned away counts the same, so it costs a minute, not a quarter of an hour. The checks back off because each ask costs about three of the free plan's 10,000 queue operations a day.
  - It still happens: on 2026-10-19 at 04:11 UTC an album's 12 songs were all turned away (three asks, 5 s and 15 s apart, each met the bot check), YouTube songs were left aside for a minute, and no zip followed in the log.
  - The Worker logs one line for every request, `YouTube relay: <name>: 0.3 MB in 1.2 s (queued 1400 ms, session 0, player 600, link 200, store 1900)` or `YouTube relay: <name>: turned away: Sign in to confirm you're not a bot (2.6 s; …)`, kept for three days (Cloudflare dashboard → Workers & Pages → `mors-edge` → Observability): that is where to count how often it happens. The host's log has a line for each ask that was turned away and for each time YouTube songs are left aside.
- **Where a song's time goes, and the trims** (2026-10-21, in the repository, not deployed yet). The Worker puts its own timings in its answer (`timing`: how long the request waited for R2's notice and the queue, then YouTube's session, the player, the song's link and storing it in the bucket), and the host's `song:` line joins them to its own (leaving the request, how late it saw the answer, collecting the song): `put 0.12s, queue 1.4s, worker 3.1s [session 0.30s, player 0.60s, link 0.20s, store 1.9s], seen +0.60s, get 0.40s` ([the cloud host](cloud.md), "Timelines"). Trimmed: the host looks for the answer every 400 ms (it waited 0.75, then 1.1, 1.7, 2.5 s and up to 3 s, so a song ready at 3.7 s was seen at 6.1 s) and cleans up without waiting; the request's name says what it asks for (`<video id>-<random>-<most bytes>`), so the Worker doesn't read or delete it (the body still says the same, for a Worker deployed before this); the Worker keeps a YouTube session for half an hour (a new one after a bot check) instead of a round trip for one before every song; an album's songs come three at a time, and one at a time once YouTube has turned the relay away during it.
- **An album opened on YouTube Music** (when Zemer doesn't have it, or can't be reached): its page gives its artists, cover and every song in order (a long album's later pages are followed). Each song must pass the list on its own, with the album's artists standing in for a song that names none, as in Zemer. Either way, the songs that pass are kept in the catalog with their artists' ids, so each is checked again against the current list just before the zip is made.
- **The zip** is made by the runner (`src/runner.ts`) from the album's songs, downloaded a few at a time, and `src/library/zip.ts` (a small writer: files stored as they are, since audio doesn't compress, UTF-8 names so Hebrew titles survive). It is sent as an attachment (`m.file`), named `Artist - Album.zip`, with the songs inside as `01 Title.m4a`. Each song is written to disk as it arrives, the zip is made of those files and streamed to the homeserver, so it is never in memory ([the cloud host](cloud.md), "Albums and the bridge"). Over `MAX_ZIP_MB` (100) it is split into parts at song boundaries, "(part 1 of 2)".
- **Not checked:** an album that comes in several pages on YouTube Music's side (more songs than one response holds; the largest album I tried, 29 songs, came in one) is followed by its continuation links, which is covered by tests but I haven't seen one live. A zip does reach the phone through the bridge (albums arrived in 30 MB parts until 2026-10-19), but an album as **one** zip of tens of MB, through the streaming patch, had not been delivered live as of 2026-10-20: the one request since the change met the bot check (above).

## Zemer's artist list

Nothing from Zemer or YouTube Music reaches the chat unless it passes Zemer's artist whitelist, the list that makes Zemer a "kosher" client. The bot reads it the way the app does, from Zemer's public content mirror (`content.zemer.io`, no sign-in): `/whitelist` (the artists, each flagged female or not, keyed by YouTube channel id, with the name in the other alphabet when there is one), `/whitelist/version` (a number that changes when the list does) and `/blockedContentIds` (single songs to hide even when their artist is listed). Today that's about 1,700 artists and 100 blocked songs.

**What passes:** a song is sent only if at least one of its credited artists is on the list, **none** of its credited artists is flagged female (a duet with a woman is out, even beside a listed man), and its id isn't on the blocked list. This is Zemer's own filter with "allow female singers" off, which is its default, plus the rule about duets. Zemer's catalog already applies the same list on its side; the bot checks again by the artists' channel ids it sends, because the owner requires the list to be applied here. There is no setting to turn it off; `YOUTUBE_MUSIC=off` turns Zemer and YouTube Music off altogether.

**It stays up to date, because the list changes through the day as music comes out:**
- Before every search the bot checks the list is no more than 5 minutes old; if it is, it asks the server (a request of about 100 bytes, waiting at most 2 seconds) and downloads the whole list only when the version number changed. The blocked songs are re-read at every check.
- While the bot runs it also checks every 10 minutes whether or not anyone searches. If the list can't be updated for a day, an alert is raised, and the last good list stays in use meanwhile.
- A song is checked again against the current list **when it is sent**, not only when it was found. So a song found yesterday stops being sent the moment its singer is flagged or the song is blocked, and a stored song with no recorded artists isn't sent at all.
- The list is kept in the catalog, so after a restart with no internet the bot still filters. With no list at all (the first run, offline) YouTube Music gives no answer, as in Zemer, and the next source is asked.

**What it doesn't cover:** only Zemer's and YouTube Music's songs are filtered. Songs from music-table.com have no artist ids to check, so they come as they always did. The list is matched by channel id, as Zemer does; when a listed artist has a second YouTube channel that isn't on the list, a few of their songs are held back (about one listed artist in twenty, in a sample). A song lists the artists YouTube credits, so a woman who isn't credited can't be caught; Zemer's blocked list is what covers those.

## What it uses

| For | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Searching | `GET https://search.zemer.io/search?q=…&k=20` (and `/artist?id=`, `/album?id=`), with `allowFemale=0&blockVideos=0&kidZone=0`. When it can't be reached: `POST /youtubei/v1/search` as the `WEB_REMIX` client with Zemer's songs filter (and its albums filter), so only songs come back: title, artists, album, length and a cover. No session id is sent with a search (Zemer found a stale one makes YouTube answer with nothing). |
| New releases | `GET https://flipphoneguy.duckdns.org/?page=zemer_releases` (see [New releases from Zemer](#new-releases-from-zemer)), with `If-None-Match`. Then the same album pages as above when a release is opened. |
| A session | `POST /youtubei/v1/visitor_id`, once. The player refuses to answer ("confirm you're not a bot") without one; a refused one is replaced once. |
| Playing a song | `POST /youtubei/v1/player` as the `VISIONOS` client, version 1.02, then 1.03 and 1.01 if that one has been retired. This is the first fallback Zemer tries: its audio links come ready to use, with no signature to solve and no PoToken to mint. The best AAC stream is taken (itag 140, 128 kbps), never an Opus one, because Google Messages plays AAC. |
| The file | A plain download from YouTube's media servers (`*.googlevideo.com`, https only), sent with the client's own User-Agent and `Range: bytes=0-`, which those servers insist on. On the cloud host the player and this download are the relay's (above). |

**Dead links, and the 403.** The media server serves the first megabyte of any link, but about one link in fifteen (measured October 2026) is "walled": it answers 403 to any request that reaches further, which is every whole-song download. Those links want a token this client can't make. So before a link is used, `YouTubeMusic` makes the very request the download will make (`Range: bytes=0-`), reads only its status and drops the body; a 403 means that link is discarded and a new one is asked for, from the next client version, then from a fresh session, up to nine tries. Songs of a megabyte or less are inside the free part and are not checked. Zemer does the same (it validates each stream and falls through to the next client). Only when every try is refused does the chat hear "YouTube Music wouldn't give me that song (its link was refused)".

A song is stored in the catalog as `https://music.youtube.com/watch?v=<id>`, never as a stream link: those expire and belong to the connection that asked, so a fresh one is asked for when the song is checked or sent (and reused for 30 minutes, so checking and then sending asks once). If the media server turns a link down, one new link is asked for before giving up.

## What it doesn't use

Zemer has three more ways to play a song (`WEB_REMIX`, `WEB_CREATOR`, `TVHTML5_SIMPLY`). They need a JavaScript player to decipher the links and a BotGuard PoToken to get past the first megabyte, which means a headless browser. They aren't here. Neither are Zemer's other services (its radio, home rows, genres, playlists, podcasts) or its relay `stream.zemer.io` (above). The one exception is its [new-releases feed](#new-releases-from-zemer).

## What you get

YouTube serves the AAC audio as a fragmented MP4 (the pieces it streams one after another). Before a song is sent, `unfragment` in `src/library/mp4.ts` puts it back together as an ordinary `.m4a` (one index with every sample in its tables, then all the audio in one piece; the audio bytes, the codec's settings and the encoder's delay at the start are kept: a real song decoded to exactly the same audio before and after, checked with ffmpeg on 2026-10-18), and then its tags and cover are written in like any song's ([The library](library.md): artists, album, number, year, the album's cover). A song that can't be put back together goes as YouTube served it.

## Turning it off, and the order

- `YOUTUBE_MUSIC=off` stops all use of Zemer and YouTube Music (and of Zemer's list). Songs already in the catalog from them then can't be sent.
- `ZEMER_SEARCH=off` stops asking Zemer's catalog; searches then use YouTube Music's own search (with no artist pages). `ZEMER_SEARCH_URL` points it somewhere else (the tests).
- `SONG_SOURCES=music-table,youtube-music` asks music-table.com first (`zemer` is another name for `youtube-music`). The default is `youtube-music,music-table`: Zemer before your catalog and music-table.com. Whichever is asked first answers if it finds anything that fits; the other is only used when it finds nothing, or can't be reached.

## It can break

This is an app API, not an official one, and YouTube changes it. The client versions in `src/sources/youtube-music.ts` are Zemer's; YouTube retires them one at a time (visionOS `0.1` stopped working on 2026-10-07 while `1.02` carried on). When every one is refused the bot says "YouTube Music wouldn't give me that song (YouTube Music answered HTTP 400)", and the fix is to copy the current `VISIONOS` versions from Zemer's `innertube/src/main/kotlin/com/metrolist/innertube/models/YouTubeClient.kt`. Nothing in the catalog is lost.

## Rights

These are YouTube Music's streams, fetched the way its app fetches them, which YouTube's terms don't allow outside its own apps. Whether you may keep or share the songs is between you and the rights holders; this project only fetches what you ask for, into your own chat.
